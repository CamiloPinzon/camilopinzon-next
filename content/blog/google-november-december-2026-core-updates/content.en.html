What happened

On November 24, 2026, Google said its November core update, which started on the 10th, had finished rolling out. An hour later it announced the next one, due in December. It hasn't given an exact day yet.

What it means

A core update is a broad change to how Google decides which results come first. Google compares it to a list of favorite restaurants that you reorder a while later: new places have opened and you've changed your mind about others. A restaurant that drops down the list didn't turn bad. Others now rank above it.

These changes don't target a particular site or page. Google says that, in general, most sites don't need to worry and may not even notice.

As for December, Google says the update aims to understand and rank content better, including for longer and more complex questions, and to improve personalized results for people who have personalization turned on. It applies in every country and language where Google Search works.

How it affects you

The good part. The November update is over, so you can now compare before and after. And if your content answers what people are looking for, a change like this can also move you up.

The risk. You can lose positions, and visits with them, without having touched your site. Google doesn't guarantee that your fixes will show. Some take effect in days and others take several months.

Who should care most. Businesses that get many of their customers from Google. If yours come mostly from referrals or social media, a quick check is enough.

What you can do

You'll use Search Console, Google's free tool that shows how people find you in Search.

  1. Wait until December 2. Google recommends letting at least a full week pass after an update finishes.
  2. Compare two weeks. We'd use November 25 to December 1 against November 3 to 9, the week before the update started. Keep in mind that Black Friday falls in the first one and can move your traffic on its own.
  3. Look at your top pages and searches. Slipping from position 2 to 4 is a small drop, and Google advises against drastic changes. Going from 4 to 29 is a large one and deserves a closer look.
  4. Check each search type separately: web, images, video and news. That tells you where the drop happened.
  5. If the drop is large and it lasts, assess your content honestly. Ask whether it really helps the people who land on it, and get an opinion from someone outside your business.
  6. Skip the quick fixes, such as removing something from a page because you heard it hurts your ranking. Deleting content is a last resort.

If your traffic is stable, you don't have to do anything. Save the numbers so you have something to compare against after the December update.

On November 12, in the middle of this period, Google also changed the AI model behind its search engine, so a swing in your traffic may not come from the update alone. We wrote about it in our post on Google's new search model.
