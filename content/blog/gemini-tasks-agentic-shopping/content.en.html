What happened

On November 17, 2026, Google added a new mode called "Task" to Gemini, its AI assistant. Gemini no longer just answers questions. It now gets things done: it calls businesses to book appointments, sorts through bills, and buys products with Google Pay.

Google says it's rolling out free to hundreds of millions of people in the US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India, with some advanced features starting in the US only. Calling is one of them: according to Google's help page, it only works in the US and only to US numbers for now. The announcement doesn't say which countries get purchases.

The same day, Google Wallet, Google's app for cards, tickets, and receipts, came to iPhone in the US. Google pitches it as the place where people keep track of what Gemini bought for them.

What it means

Picture your customer hiring a personal assistant who never gets tired of waiting on hold. They say "book the nearest auto shop for early Friday morning," and the assistant looks up shops on Google Maps, calls two at once, asks around, picks one, and puts the appointment on the calendar. That's the example Google itself gives.

When Gemini calls, it opens by saying it's an AI assistant from Google, that the line is recorded, and who it's calling for. It can't pay over the phone or share card numbers, passwords, or health information. There's also a daily cap on how many calls each person can have it make.

Purchases happen somewhere else: on the web, through Google Pay. To pay, Gemini uses AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol), a standard that lets an AI agent pay on someone's behalf. The real card number never travels. A stand-in number goes instead, which payments people call a "token." Shoppers can set a hard spending cap and still earn their points, cashback, and card protections. Gemini asks for confirmation before it clicks "Buy," except for routine purchases the user has set to "Always allow."

How it affects you

The upside. If you take bookings by phone (auto shops, clinics, salons, restaurants), you may hear from people who never would have called because they were too busy or couldn't be bothered. And if you sell online, a shopper can ask Gemini to compare products and buy one. To compare, Gemini uses the Shopping Graph, the product catalog Google builds from stores and ads.

What to watch for. Google says it outright: on some sites, Gemini gets most of the way through checkout and then hands the screen back to the shopper for the final click. Its explanation is that for decades bots were blocked by default because most of them were malicious. If your bot protection or fraud system is strict, it may stop these purchases. The shopper would have to finish by hand, and not everyone will.

One detail some store owners won't love: in Google's example, Gemini found a discount code and saved the shopper $10. If you have coupon codes floating around the internet that you never meant for everyone, an assistant that goes looking for them will find them.

Who isn't affected yet. If your business and your customers are outside those six countries, nothing changes for now. Calls will only reach you if you have a US number, and they don't work in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, or Nebraska.

Our take: this is the second big company in a month to let an assistant shop for people, and this time it's free and in several countries. You don't need to rebuild your store. You do need to make sure you're not shutting the door on a customer who shows up in a new way.

What you can do

  1. Give whoever answers your phone a heads-up. AI assistants may call and will say so. Treat them like any other customer: share hours, prices, and openings. Gemini doesn't take payments or ask for card details over the phone, so a "Google" call asking for them is a red flag.
  2. Check your Google Business Profile: hours, phone number, and services. Gemini finds businesses on Google Maps, so if that info is wrong, the call or the booking will be too.
  3. Ask your developers to test checkout the way an agent would. Have them check whether your bot protection (CAPTCHA, firewall, or fraud tool) turns away legitimate purchases, and whether carts are being dropped right at the last step.
  4. If you'd rather not get these calls, Google has a page to block calls from Gemini. You sign in with your Google account or verify your phone number, and you can opt back in anytime.
  5. If you run an app or a SaaS product, Google is taking applications to connect it to Gemini (the Connected Apps Platform). More than 60 apps are already connected, Instacart among them.
